\begin{abstract}
Object-hallucination benchmarks check the objects a vision-language model names against those
annotated in the image, and a better score is read as better visual grounding. The natural check is
to remove the image and ask whether the gain survives. We show that the answer depends on which
ablation is used, and that the usual choice --- a blank field --- is often the wrong one. We begin
\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}'s description of an image with a passage cut from human captions of other
images, score the objects that passage names, and vary only whether those captions come from one
other scene or five: the present-minus-absent gap rises by $0.49$ on a $[-1, 1]$ scale. With the
image replaced by a uniform grey field and every token of text unchanged it still rises by $0.50$, a
blind-to-sighted ratio of $1.03$ ($95\%$ CI $0.92$--$1.16$); with a mismatched real image instead
only $0.33$ survives, and $0.16$ \civ{0.11}{0.21} of the rise is attributable to the image. The
blank conceals this by depressing the one-scene hit rate from $0.68$ to $0.47$, preserving the
difference while destroying the level. Nor is a blank merely the most severe ablation: a heavy blur
moves the image further in embedding distance yet leaves POPE at $J = +0.19$ where grey leaves $0$,
because grey empties CHAIR's denominator and makes POPE's labels false, returning no data rather
than a null. Run with a mismatched image on official POPE the control is informative, and a
published method passes it --- PAI's gain is image-attributable, $+0.021$ \civ{+0.004}{+0.037}. On
\textsc{Qwen3-VL-8B} part of our own rise needs the image, $+0.060$ \civ{+0.024}{+0.096}, which
bounds the claim; the rise is also specific to assembled captions and to free generation. Throughout,
the scalar hides its parts, since the image's effects on the two rates cancel inside it. We recommend
reporting both rates with their decomposition, blinding every endpoint whose candidates the input
supplies, and choosing an ablation the model can still answer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When a vision-language model scores better on an object-hallucination benchmark, the improvement is
usually read as better visual grounding: the model's descriptions now track the image more closely.
Training-free mitigation methods report better scores on these benchmarks as reduced hallucination
\citep{pai, vista, adaiat, regionaware, vcd}, some explicitly as better grounding
\citep{cai, savaa}. Yet the scores are also sensitive to caption length and generation budget
\citep{pope, regionaware}, and lower CHAIR can accompany less informative output
\citep{safefaith}: they move when a model becomes more or less willing to name objects, and nothing
in a score's value says which happened. This paper asks when the two can be told apart.

The answer depends on whether the model keeps responding about the scored objects once the image is
gone. POPE \citep{pope} names each candidate object in a question, and the endpoint we build here
names each one in text the model is continuing, so a hit rate $H$ on present candidates and a
false-alarm rate $F$ on absent ones stay measurable whatever the model sees. CHAIR \citep{chair}
scores only what the model chooses to mention: its instance score is the share of those mentions that
name absent objects, a false-discovery rate \citep{throne} whose denominator the model chooses. Fixing a candidate
list is not enough: recoding CHAIR over all $80$ COCO categories fixes the candidates, but a model
that names nothing leaves both rates at zero, and one that affirms everything sets both to one ---
either corner sends $J$ and $d'$ to zero. Both occur in published work: blinded \textsc{LLaVA-1.5}
answers ``no'' to everything \citep{lan2026seeing}, sighted \textsc{MultiModal-GPT} ``yes''
\citep{pope}. Supplied candidates make the check \emph{runnable}, not automatically informative.

We build an endpoint with fixed candidates and give it a manipulation whose route is known to be
textual. We ask the model to describe an image and start its answer for it with a passage cut from
five human captions of other COCO images, varying only whether the captions describe one other scene
or five. The endpoint is the rate at which the model's continuation names an object from that passage
when the object is in the image ($H$), minus the rate when it is not ($F$). On
\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} the score rises by $0.49$ on its $[-1, 1]$ scale, carried mainly by a fall in
$F$ from $0.49$ to $0.11$ --- the profile a benchmark reader would take as improved grounding. With the
image replaced by a uniform grey field of the same size and every token of text unchanged, the rise is
$0.50$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lead}). The image is used, raising the score by about $0.2$ in each condition,
but against this ablation the rise itself does not need it. The same holds on
\textsc{LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B}, and on \textsc{Kosmos-2} the rise is larger without the image; a less
destructive ablation and a fourth architecture each put part of it back (Section~\ref{sec:blind}).

Neither the score nor its standard decomposition can tell these cases apart. $H - F$ is Youden's
$J$ \citeyearpar{youden1950}, which fuses how separable the two populations are with where the
responder sets its threshold \citep{snodgrass1988}, and as a difference it hides offsetting movements:
here the image changes both $H$ and $F$, in ways that cancel in $J$. Recoding the rates post hoc as a
signal-detection separation $d'$ and threshold $c$ does not separate the cases either, because $d'$
rises by $1.53$ with the image and $1.35$ without it. Separability is measured from behaviour over the
objects put up for scoring, and nothing in it records whether the evidence came from the image or from
the text that chose those objects. Only the blind arm tells them apart.

\paragraph{Why a constructed manipulation.} The scene-count manipulation is not a model of any
mitigation method. It answers a narrower question under the strictest control we could build: can a
present-minus-absent score register a large, replicating rise that does not need the image? That
requires a manipulation with a known textual route and a control that removes the image while
leaving every token of text in place. The answer is yes, on this construction; the rise itself does not appear when the
passage is natural prose. It does not show that any published gain takes a similar
route. Section~\ref{sec:mitigation} gives one data point: on standard CHAIR, PAI
lowers $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$ by $5.6$ points while the rate of naming present objects falls by seven ---
a trade-off \citet{sparc} already report --- and shortening the unmodified model's own captions to
PAI's false-alarm rate gives nearly the same $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$. That pattern is consistent with a threshold shift, although a registered
test could not resolve whether PAI also changes separation. A uniform field cannot run the blind control on
CHAIR, but a graded one can, and under it PAI's gain is image-attributable on CHAIR and POPE alike.

\paragraph{What is new.} Measuring what a score owes to the image is not ours. Question-only baselines
exposed language priors in VQA \citep{goyal2017vqa, agrawal2018vqacp}, MMStar reports a model's score
with the image minus its score without it \citep{mmstar}, \citet{lan2026seeing} ask whether
vision-language benchmarks can be answered without the image; \citet{zafar2026medical} derive
grounding metrics from blank and image-absent inputs; \citet{liao2026decodable} find a training-free
gain of $19$ points with a grey blank against $21$ with the image and propose that control as a
default for steering claims; and \citet{pai} themselves generate with the image excluded to diagnose
text inertia. \citet{safefaith} argue that hallucination reduction often reflects fewer visual
claims rather than better grounding. We contribute three things. (i) The endpoint-level condition
above: an image-ablated control is informative only if the model responds non-degenerately about the
scored objects without the image, which candidates supplied in the input make possible and a
model-chosen denominator does not. (ii) A controlled demonstration, on an assembled-caption passage,
two \textsc{LLaVA}-family models and \textsc{Kosmos-2} (on which the rise is larger without the
image), that a large rise in a present-minus-absent score need not require the image, with a decomposition showing that
the image's effects on the two rates cancel in the score and that the separation term survives
blinding. (iii) The same decomposition on a published method under standard CHAIR, and a reporting
format that exposes what a single score hides.

\paragraph{Scope.} We do not claim that these benchmarks are invalid, that any published gain is
misattributed, or that models ignore the image. We claim that a change in a present-minus-absent
score does not identify a change in visual grounding when the intervention can also change which
objects the model names, and we say which endpoints allow that to be checked. Our evidence of the
failure is confined to free generation and to assembled-caption passages.

\section{What a present-minus-absent score can identify}
\label{sec:theory}

Write $H$ for the rate at which a model names a candidate object that is present in the image and
$F$ for the rate at which it names one that is absent. Scores built on a fixed candidate set are
functions of $(H, F)$; the simplest is $J = H - F$, which has the algebraic form of Youden's $J$, a
hit rate minus a false-alarm rate over disjoint populations. We borrow the correspondence for its
decomposition, not to claim that captioning is a diagnostic test (Appendix~\ref{app:sdt}).

CHAIR has a different form. With $P$ present and $A$ absent candidates, and counting mention
occurrences as our CHAIR port does (Appendix~\ref{app:mitig-rates}),
$\mathrm{CHAIR}_i = \kappa FA/(HP + \kappa FA)$, where $\kappa$ is the number of mentions per named
absent object divided by the number per named present object ($\kappa = 1$ if each named object is
counted once). It falls whenever $\kappa F$ falls proportionally faster than $H$, including when $H$
falls too, so CHAIR can improve while $J$ worsens.

\subsection{Why the score does not isolate sensitivity}

Under an equal-variance Gaussian formulation the two rates give a separation and a threshold,
\begin{equation}
d' = Z(H) - Z(F), \qquad c = -\tfrac{1}{2}\left[Z(H) + Z(F)\right],
\end{equation}
with $Z = \Phi^{-1}$. Inverting gives $H = \Phi(d'/2 - c)$ and $F = \Phi(-d'/2 - c)$, so
\begin{equation}
J \;=\; \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{d'}{2} - c\right) - \Phi\!\left(-\tfrac{d'}{2} - c\right),
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
and differentiating in the threshold,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial J}{\partial c} \;=\; \varphi\!\left(-\tfrac{d'}{2} - c\right) - \varphi\!\left(\tfrac{d'}{2} - c\right),
\label{eq:dJdc}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi$ the standard normal density. Equation~\ref{eq:dJdc} vanishes only where $c\,d' = 0$, so
for any non-zero $d'$ the score depends on the threshold. A reader who sees $J$ move cannot tell
whether $d'$ moved, $c$ moved, or both --- and, since $J$ is a difference, cannot tell whether an
unchanged $J$ hides opposite movements in $H$ and $F$.

That half of the argument is familiar. The consequential half is that $d'$ does not resolve the
problem either. It measures how well behaviour separates the two populations using whatever
information the model has, and the text in the prompt --- including which objects it puts up for
scoring --- is part of that information. Nothing in Equation~\ref{eq:J} distinguishes separability
achieved by inspecting the image from separability achieved through the text.

\paragraph{Image use is not image attribution.} A model can use the image --- its $d'$ falling when the
image is removed --- while a particular \emph{change} in the score owes nothing to the image.
Establishing the second requires a condition in which the image carries no information, everything
else is held fixed, and the model still responds about every scored object.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stimulus.} We ask the model to describe the image and pre-fill the start of its answer
with a passage built from five human COCO captions of other images: in \texttt{cap1} all five
describe one other image, in \texttt{cap5} five different images. They are shuffled, joined and
truncated to a per-cell token length --- on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}, the smaller of half the model's own
caption length and the length of a natural-prose description of the first source image, mean $44.6$
tokens (Appendix~\ref{app:setup} gives each model's rule and prompt) --- and the model continues greedily
for up to $384$ tokens. Both conditions have identical passage length, so the
contrast is never a passage-length contrast; continuations do differ in length, and on
\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} $45\%$ of one-scene and $68\%$ of five-scene continuations reach the budget. After truncation the passage covers $1.0$ versus about $2.8$ distinct scenes and
roughly twice as many distinct object types.

\paragraph{Endpoint.} The scored unit is (image, object type named in the passage), and carry-forward
is the event that the model's continuation names that type. $H$ and $F$ are carry-forward rates over
units whose type is and is not in the image's ground-truth set, and the primary contrast $\dPrim$ is
the change in $J$ from \texttt{cap1} to \texttt{cap5}. The endpoint, and the aggregation across cuts
used for pooled estimates, were chosen after seeing data, when CHAIR proved structurally unusable on
these conditions (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}).

\paragraph{Blind condition.} The same pipeline with the image replaced by a flat mid-grey rectangle at
the target's pixel dimensions and every token of text unchanged. The blind arm therefore scores the
identical units with identical labels --- on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}, $528$ present and $2{,}332$ absent
in \texttt{cap1}, $892$ and $4{,}988$ in \texttt{cap5} --- and the image-attributable component is
$\dSight = \dPrim - \dBlind$. A uniform field is out of distribution for the image encoder, so the
blind arm shows what the score does when the image carries no information, not what an unchanged
visual representation would do.

\paragraph{Inference and registration.} Intervals are $95\%$ paired bootstraps clustered on the
image. Analyses were pre-registered with their decision rules before data collection except where
marked; Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} lists every registered analysis and its outcome in one table. We
read $\dSight$ against a reference margin of $\pm 0.10$, about a fifth of the largest effect under
test; the margin is our reporting choice, not a registered bound.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Experiment 1: a large apparent grounding change}
\label{sec:exp1}

On \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} the score rises from $+0.19$ to $+0.67$, a contrast of $+0.49$
\civ{+0.43}{+0.54}, or $+0.36$ \civ{+0.30}{+0.41} when continuations are length-matched. It rises monotonically across a
five-point grid in the number of scenes, though that grid was not run blind and cannot separate
object types from mentions per type. With length matched, $H$ barely moves ($-0.03$
\civ{-0.08}{+0.02}) while $F$ falls sixfold, from $0.46$ to $0.08$. On \textsc{LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B}
the contrast is $+0.38$ \civ{+0.32}{+0.43} and survives object-type matching, removal of empty
continuations, a seed change and removal of duplicated passages (Appendix~\ref{app:models}).

\paragraph{The rise is specific to assembled captions.} A registered version of the manipulation
that builds the passage from natural prose (Localized Narratives descriptions) instead of COCO captions
was null ($+0.01$ \civ{-0.04}{+0.05}), and so was every specification of it, including the
unit-standardised control its failed units-seen gate required ($-0.05$ \civ{-0.14}{+0.03}) and a
restriction to cells where no continuation reached the budget. That version realised about half the headline's dose
(one against about two scenes, not one against $2.8$), so its null is weaker than a null at full
dose; by its registration, the lead result is specific to the assembled-caption construction. An
earlier registered run drew natural prose from five non-redundant sources: its gap
($+0.44$ \civ{+0.40}{+0.48}) exceeds single-source prose ($+0.37$) by $+0.07$ \civ{+0.02}{+0.13} raw
but $+0.02$ \civ{-0.03}{+0.07} length-matched, and separates neither mechanism it was built to test.

\subsection{Experiment 2: the rise under image ablation}
\label{sec:blind}

Table~\ref{tab:models} gives the contrast with the image and under grey on four architectures. On
both \textsc{LLaVA} models $\dSight$ contains zero and excludes an image contribution of $0.10$
either way, and the blind rise on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} is $1.03$ \civ{0.92}{1.16} times the sighted

from both sides: on \textsc{Kosmos-2} the rise is larger without the image, while on

$\pm 0.10$ margin, so $90\%$ of that rise still survives blinding, with the degeneracy screen passing
on every arm.

On \textsc{Kosmos-2} the component remains $-0.09$ \civ{-0.15}{-0.03} under within-pair length
matching, though its blind continuations run about $1.7$ times longer with no cross-arm match, and
that run proceeded under an amended stop gate whose two failing thresholds were revised before any

\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} data (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). \textsc{Qwen2.5-VL-7B} produced four- or five-token
continuations in every arm, sighted included, and is not interpreted, as its registration
pre-committed (Appendix~\ref{app:models}). \textsc{LLaVA-OV-0.5B}'s blind arms are also short
(medians of five and eleven tokens, against $192$ sighted), so its blind rise is the thinner of the two
\textsc{LLaVA} replications.

\paragraph{The image is used, and the design sees it.} Within each condition the image raises $J$ by
$+0.21$ \civ{+0.16}{+0.26} and $+0.19$ \civ{+0.16}{+0.23} on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}, and by $+0.16$
\civ{+0.11}{+0.20} and $+0.12$ \civ{+0.09}{+0.15} on \textsc{LLaVA-OV-0.5B}, and it raises $d'$ in every
condition with every interval excluding zero (Table~\ref{tab:dprime}). For $J$ that contribution
is nearly equal across conditions, which is why $\dSight$ is null. For $H$ and $F$ separately it is
not (Table~\ref{tab:report}), and the two differences cancel in $J$. \textsc{Kosmos-2}'s component,
which excludes zero, shows the estimand is not held at zero by construction.

\paragraph{A less destructive ablation disagrees.} Grey asks whether the rise needs visual input at
all. Substituting a \emph{different real image} --- prefix tokens identical, the model fluent about the
substituted scene and by every absolute measure less degenerate than the grey arm --- asks whether it
needs \emph{this} image, and answers differently: $\dSight$ is $+0.16$ \civ{+0.11}{+0.21}, a
blind-to-sighted ratio of $0.67$ \civ{0.57}{0.77} whose interval excludes grey's $1.03$. All nine pixel ablations
side with grey, including four that destroy more of the image than the substitution does. The disagreement is entirely in the hit rate --- image-attributable $\Delta H$ of
$+0.15$ \civ{+0.10}{+0.20} against $-0.01$ \civ{-0.03}{+0.01} for $F$ --- because grey depresses the
one-scene hit rate to $0.47$ against $0.68$ sighted, inflating the rise that remains, whereas under a
wrong image it starts at $0.64$ and \emph{falls} to $0.60$. About a third of the rise needs the
correct image, and the blank-image control does not show it: a blank answers a weaker question than
it appears to.

\paragraph{Unit composition.} The two conditions score different units, and present units are
enriched for objects the model names anyway ($90\%$ already appear in its no-passage caption), so
composition is a non-visual route to a higher score in either arm and plausibly contributes to both
rises: stratifying by object base rate leaves $+0.33$ \civ{+0.22}{+0.44} of $+0.49$, and we did not
report the blind rise within strata (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Because both arms score identical
units with identical labels, composition cancels from $\dSight$ to first order.

\paragraph{Robustness.} $\dSight$ stays between $-0.04$ and $-0.02$ under three ground-truth
constructions and contains zero under every registered truncation rule and a length match. The
registered mention-rate control does not pass cleanly, failing on five of sixteen powered subsets on
\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}, so the null on $J$ pools heterogeneous cells; the smallest powered
blind-to-sighted ratio is $0.73$, near the mismatch arm's $0.67$ (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{When the question supplies the object.}\label{sec:forced} Posed as a pre-registered
yes/no probe on the identical units, the scene-count contrast is $-0.01$ \civ{-0.03}{+0.01} against
$+0.49$ in free generation (Table~\ref{tab:register}); on that outcome the registration committed us
to confine every claim to free generation, and we do. The probe saturates and two non-saturating
graded readouts are also null (Appendix~\ref{app:unsat}).

\section{A published method on standard CHAIR}
\label{sec:mitigation}

We applied the same decomposition to PAI \citep{pai}, a training-free method that amplifies attention
to image tokens during decoding, on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} captioning $500$ COCO images at its
prescribed strength. Unlike Section~\ref{sec:decomp} this decomposition was registered in advance,
with a $\pm 0.15$ equivalence margin on $\Delta d'$ and its logit companion and a $0.15$ materiality
threshold on $\Delta c$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mitig}). The scored units are all $80$ COCO categories per
image, so $H$ and $F$ are defined; because $F$ is about $0.01$, $J$ is essentially $H$ and $d'$ is
large.

PAI lowers $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$ from $12.8$ to $7.2$ (Table~\ref{tab:pai}). That PAI buys precision with recall is
already established: \citet{sparc} report precision rising from $84.70$ to $90.64$ and recall falling
from $79.46$ to $72.44$ for PAI on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5} over $500$ COCO images. Our $H$ is that recall,
and it falls by $0.07$ against their $0.0702$ --- a check on the recoding, not a finding of ours.
Taking the trade-off as given, the decomposition adds this: the rate of naming absent objects also
halves, so CHAIR improves while $J$ worsens by $0.07$, the two summaries ordering the same captions
oppositely. Probit $d'$
does not move and the threshold does (Figure~\ref{fig:hf}; Appendix~\ref{app:zroc}): moving $c$ alone accounts for a change
in $J$ of $-0.07$, moving $d'$ alone for $+0.003$. Truncation is already a standard length baseline
\citep{lessismore}; matching instead on the false-alarm rate, shortening the unmodified model's own
captions to PAI's $F$ gives $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$ of $8.0$ \civ{6.9}{9.3}, a difference from PAI of
$-0.8$ \civ{-1.8}{+0.4}; the shortened captions name present objects at $0.684$ against PAI's
$0.708$ \civ{-0.004}{+0.051}, straddling the registered $\pm 0.03$ margin, so that comparison is
unresolved too. Shortening is not itself a pure threshold change, so it places PAI near the
unmodified model's trajectory rather than on it.

Whether a small separation gain accompanies the shift is unresolved: a log-odds measure rises
($+0.30$ \civ{+0.13}{+0.48}) while probit $d'$ and the matched-$F$ comparison do not, and one
operating point cannot separate these readings, so the registered primary returns
\textsc{cannot-resolve}.

No uniform field can run the control here: under grey, and equally under flat black,
\textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B} named none of the $80$ categories in any of $500$ captions, with or without
PAI, so $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$ is $0/0$ and the arm returns no data. Every \emph{graded} degradation leaves it
intact, however --- $378$ to $500$ of $500$ captions still name a category --- so the control is
runnable after all, and PAI passes it on CHAIR too. Its $\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$
gain of $+5.6$ \civ{+3.9}{+7.2} falls to $+0.4$ \civ{-1.2}{+2.0} when the caption is generated from
a mismatched image, leaving $+5.2$ \civ{+2.9}{+7.4} attributable to the image; a heavy blur agrees
($+8.7$ \civ{+2.6}{+15.0}). That arm is algebraically our registered permuted-ground-truth
secondary here, so the two are one test.

\paragraph{The blind control on a standard benchmark, and the method passes it.} We ran on official
POPE the test this paper recommends. With a mismatched real image as the ablation, PAI's
attention-only configuration --- the one that reproduces its published POPE gain, $+1.10$ accuracy
points against their $+1.06$ --- raises $J$ by $+0.022$ \civ{+0.011}{+0.034} sighted against $+0.001$
\civ{-0.010}{+0.012} ablated, so $\Delta_{\text{image}}$ is $+0.021$ \civ{+0.004}{+0.037} and excludes
zero. That gain does need the image, and our registered rule returns \textsc{partial} only because
the lower bound falls just below its half-gain line. Under a grey field the same test is
uninformative for a different reason: the model answers ``no'' to all $9{,}000$ questions, correctly,
so $H = F = 0$ by construction and the screen returns undefined. The released
\texttt{--use-attn --use-cfg} configuration shows no gain at all ($\Delta_{\text{image}} = +0.000$),
because it suppresses the amplification at the prefill row that carries a one-token answer.

The same signature appears in PAI's own published POPE table, which needs no new compute to
decompose: on balanced splits $H$ is the reported recall and $F = H + 1 - 2\,\mathrm{acc}$, giving
$\Delta d' = +0.04$ against $\Delta c = +0.18$ where Table~\ref{tab:pai} gives $+0.02$ against
$+0.24$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mitig}).

On a standard benchmark, then, a trade-off that its own literature already reports \citep{sparc}
reads, once decomposed, as a shift in where the model sets its threshold rather than in how well it
separates present from absent objects, landing near what shortening the unmodified model's captions
achieves. Nothing here says PAI's improvement is spurious --- it is tied to the image --- only that
$\mathrm{CHAIR}_i$ alone cannot say which kind of improvement it is, and that the rates which can are
already computed wherever CHAIR is.

\section{Recommended reporting}
\label{sec:reco}

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0.1em
\item \textbf{Report $H$ and $F$ with their decomposition, not a single score.} Report $J$ with the
image-attributable part of each rate, since a null on $J$ can hide offsetting effects on the two rates,
and $d'$ and $c$ where the question is separation against threshold (Table~\ref{tab:report}). For CHAIR, report the $H$ and $F$ its
mention records already contain (Table~\ref{tab:pai}).
\item \textbf{Blind every endpoint whose candidates are supplied in the input, and choose the
ablation deliberately.} A question naming each object, as in POPE, or a passage naming it, as here,
asks the model about every scored object without the image; check the answers are not degenerate
before reading the result. Severity is not informativeness: over nine pixel ablations,
how much of the image is destroyed and how much $\dSight$ recovers are unrelated (Spearman $+0.10$),
and four of them --- grey and flat black among them --- destroy \emph{more} than the mismatched real
scene that is the only ablation to expose a contribution. Grey also empties CHAIR's denominator and
falsifies POPE's labels. Report an interval on $\dSight$, say which ablation produced it, and prefer
one that supplies a coherent alternative the model can still answer.
\item \textbf{Do not read an endpoint whose denominator the model chooses as measuring grounding on
its own} whenever the intervention can change how readily the model names objects --- attention or
activation interventions, contrastive or shortened decoding, instruction changes. Report its $H$ and
$F$, and say that its attribution to the image is untested.
\end{enumerate}

Published tables often already contain the rates: on POPE's balanced splits $H$ is the reported
recall and $F = H + 1 - 2\,\mathrm{acc}$, which prices the two degenerate corners --- both at accuracy
$50.0$ and $J = 0$, but F1 $66.7$ for an always-``yes'' model against $0.0$ for a blinded one.

A present-minus-absent score cannot separate a change in separability from one in threshold, hides
offsetting movements in its two rates, and cannot say whether the image produced the separability.
An image-ablated arm can, where the ablation leaves something to score.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Object-hallucination endpoints.} CHAIR \citep{chair} scores captions against COCO
annotations \citep{coco} as the share of mentioned objects that are absent. POPE \citep{pope} fixes
the candidates and asks yes/no existence questions, drawing absent objects at random, by dataset
frequency, or by co-occurrence with present ones --- and models do hallucinate the frequent and
co-occurring ones. Our endpoint shares POPE's fixed candidates but is
scored in free generation; POPE's popular and adversarial splits are the natural template for a
variant that separates the frequency prior of Section~\ref{sec:blind} from grounding. CHAIR's own
authors found no correlation between caption length and hallucination across 2018 captioning models
\citep{chair}; later work finds CHAIR sensitive to length and generation budget in current models, and
rewarding short captions for want of a recall term \citep{pope, regionaware, throne}.

\paragraph{Image-ablated controls.} In VQA a question-only baseline reaches $48.2\%$ against $60.4\%$
\citep{goyal2017vqa}, and accuracy degrades sharply when answer priors shift \citep{agrawal2018vqacp}. MMStar's multi-modal gain is the score with the image minus the score
without it, using a grey image for two models that could not run image-free \citep{mmstar}. \citet{lan2026seeing}, whose blinded \textsc{LLaVA-1.5} we
return to in Section~\ref{sec:reco}, degrade the image across four benchmarks and ask whether the
items can be answered without looking, as do \citet{kaduri2025whats} by ablating the
attention path rather than the pixels. That is a question about benchmarks; ours is about
interventions --- is a \emph{change} in score attributable to the image, and on which endpoints can
that be checked?
\citet{zafar2026medical} derive grounding metrics from blank and image-absent inputs, and
\citet{liao2026decodable} find a steering gain on camera-relative direction judgements that survives a
grey blank almost intact. AMBER reports coverage beside its hallucination rate
\citep{amber}, and \citet{safefaith} find that across six methods lower CHAIR goes with lower recall,
a pattern they call risk suppression; they run no image-ablated condition and do not decompose an
effect. Our PAI result decomposes an instance of that pattern into separation and threshold.

\paragraph{Signal detection and construct validity.} Separating sensitivity from threshold is the
founding move of signal-detection theory, and reporting a corrected score without its components
conflates them \citep{snodgrass1988, youden1950}; applying it to language models is an active line
\citep{llmsdt2026, llmmetacog2026, machinepsych2026}, in each case with a text model's own confidence
as the decision variable. Nearest to us, \citet{mirage} argue that contrastive decoding's POPE gains
are a response shift rather than better grounding, since their own non-visual manipulations match or
beat VCD ($87.6$ and $89.6$ against VCD's $88.6$ and greedy's $87.1$ on \textsc{LLaVA-1.5-7B}); they
report accuracy and yes-rate without decomposing them, and run no image-ablated arm. We add a
multimodal setting in which the sensitivity term is itself contaminated, because separability can
come from the prompt rather than the image. Unlike protocol artefacts in unfair comparisons \citep{lucic, choi, dacrema, winnerscurse}, ours is an
endpoint that does not isolate its construct even under a fair one.

\paragraph{Context-induced hallucination and mitigation.} Our stimulus follows \citet{shortcut}, who
prompt a captioner with ground-truth captions of an unrelated image; we add variation in a caption
property they hold fixed, and the blind condition. Training-free methods that intervene on attention
or activations during decoding \citep{pai, vista, cai, savaa, regionaware, adaiat} or on the decoding
distribution \citep{vcd} are evaluated on the endpoints above; \citet{axbench} is the closest
methodological neighbour in intervention evaluation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{What this establishes, and what it does not.} We do not show that vision-language models
ignore images, that CHAIR or POPE are invalid, that grounding metrics are useless, or that published
hallucination reductions are threshold effects in general: Section~\ref{sec:blind} finds the image
used in every condition we measured, and Section~\ref{sec:mitigation} finds a published method's
CHAIR gain tied to image content. We do show four things. An intervention can raise a present-minus-absent
grounding score under full visual input; it raises it by the same amount when the image carries no
information, so the rise is not attributable to visual information; the image's effects on the two
rates cancel inside the score, so $H$ and $F$ carry what the scalar hides; and whether this control
can be run depends on how the endpoint chooses the objects it scores.

\textbf{The demonstration endpoint is constructed.} It was adopted after CHAIR proved unusable on these
conditions and is not a benchmark in use. We have not shown the blind control catching a
misattributed gain on a benchmark people report: we ran that test and the method passed it
(Section~\ref{sec:mitigation}). Grey falsifies an existence question's labels as surely as it empties
CHAIR's denominator, so the ablation, not the benchmark, decides whether the control can be read.
The CHAIR analysis covers one method on one model and its registered primary is unresolved.

\textbf{What the null does and does not say.} The null concerns $J$, on an assembled-caption passage;
with natural prose there is no rise to attribute. The \textsc{LLaVA-OV-0.5B} blind arms are short. The null pools
heterogeneous cells, and unit composition plausibly contributes to both rises; we did not report the blind rise within base-rate strata or build an
adversarially sampled variant. Nor do we identify what moves the score:
distinct object types double as mentions per type halve, and a registered repetition condition was
unresolved on its precision rule, repetition's share estimated at $0.23$ \civ{0.08}{0.39}.

\textbf{Controls and statistics.} The positive control is built into the data rather than designed.
The $d'$/$c$ decomposition of the main endpoint is post hoc, and its image-attributable $d'$ has
opposite signs on the two \textsc{LLaVA} models. The $\pm 0.10$ margin is a reporting choice, the dose
grid was run only with the image present, and the forced-choice readouts saturate. Our degeneracy screens
test length, not repetition: continuations loop in most five-scene cells on every model, and on
\textsc{Qwen3-VL-8B} sighted and blind loop at different rates ($0.83$ against $0.91$) in the
condition carrying its $\dSight$, with no matched contrast.

\textbf{Models and data.} Two \textsc{LLaVA} models replicate; \textsc{Kosmos-2} and
\textsc{Qwen3-VL-8B} bound the result in opposite directions; \textsc{Qwen2.5-VL-7B} is not
interpreted \citep{llava15, llavaov, kosmos2, qwen25vl}. All images are COCO \citep{coco}.
